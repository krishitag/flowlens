# FlowLens: Explainable Real-Time Network Anomaly Detection with TreeSHAP and Two-Tier Monitoring

Vedika · Krishita Gupta · Krish Singh
School of Computer Science and Engineering, Vellore Institute of Technology

## Abstract

Network monitoring tools usually separate traffic visibility from security alerting. When a machine-learning detector raises an alert, the analyst receives a label but no reason. Explainable intrusion detection work commonly attaches SHAP explanations to such alerts, but mostly offline and often with Kernel SHAP, a model-agnostic estimator that is far too slow to run per connection on live traffic. We argue that per-flow explanations are cheap enough for real-time monitoring when two design choices are combined: tree-based detectors explained with TreeSHAP, which computes exact Shapley values in polynomial time, and a two-tier architecture that runs full scoring and explanation only when traffic is abnormal. We implement this design as FlowLens, which replays labelled CIC-IDS2017 flows as a one-second-window stream. An EWMA monitor switches between NORMAL and ELEVATED states. LightGBM classifies known attack types, an Isolation Forest trained only on benign traffic serves as the detector for unseen ones, and TreeSHAP turns the top contributing features into plain-language sentences shown on a live dashboard. On the test split, LightGBM reaches a macro F1 of [lgbm_macro_f1] with a benign false-positive rate of [lgbm_benign_fpr]. TreeSHAP explains a flow in [tree_p50] ms at the median, against [kernel_p50] ms for Kernel SHAP, a [shap_speedup]× speed-up. With at most 300 explanations per window, end-to-end processing takes [p95_e2e_ms] ms per one-second window at the 95th percentile under stress load. Unseen attacks remain the weak point: with [holdout_class] withheld from training, LightGBM alone caught [holdout_lgbm_recall] of its flows, the Isolation Forest [holdout_iforest_recall] and the two together [holdout_either_recall], and with Bot withheld neither detector caught any. Low-and-slow attacks that do not change traffic volume are caught only at the sampling rate.
